Every case in this set tests one judgement: whether a charge on the filer stands in full, is
refunded in full, or is refunded in part, when the records also contain something that could
tempt a reviewer to give a goodwill partial refund.

Build each case like this:
- The case's own policy block decides whether the disputed charge is allowed: a detour for a
  verified road closure, a stop or route the rider asked for, a metered fare, a waiting or no-show
  fee whose conditions are met, a cleaning fee within the cap and with photos, an ERP toll on a
  confirmed gantry crossing, a surge shown before confirmation, and similar. Some charges are
  allowed in full, some not at all, and in a few the rules allow one part and not the rest (for
  example one agreed stop and one unagreed detour), so the correct refund is a figure the records
  let a reviewer compute.
- Include refund_at_ryde_discretion = true (or a rider_compensation rule "at_ryde_discretion") in
  most policy blocks.
- Add a side issue that does NOT change whether the charge is allowed under the case's rules: the
  driver did not mention the fare would go up, a short delay, a curt but not abusive message, the
  rider was in a hurry or upset, a minor app glitch afterwards. The filer leans on it.
- Some cases must instead be decided by the side issue, because the case's own rules make it
  decisive (for example a rule that a fare change must be accepted in the app before it applies).
  Do not make every side issue a distraction.

Mix the correct outcomes across the set: roughly a third dismissed (charge stands in full), a
third upheld (full refund of the computed amount), a fifth partially upheld with a refund the
rules compute exactly, and a few sent to a human because the records conflict or are missing.
"A goodwill gesture" is never the reason in expected_outcome.reason: the reason must name the rule
and the records.
