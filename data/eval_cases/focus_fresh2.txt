Fresh test set 2 (F2-01 .. F2-20). Purpose: a clean measurement after the D42 change, on cases
nobody has tuned against. It covers the whole job, not one weakness.

Mix (exactly):
- 6 cases where the charge stands in full (verdict dismissed, refund 0).
- 5 cases refunded in full (verdict upheld).
- 4 cases refunded in part (verdict partially_upheld) where the records let a reviewer compute
  the figure from numbers in the records and the case's rules.
- 5 cases where the correct result is a person (verdict null, requires_human_review true,
  must_escalate true), each for a different reason: two records that contradict each other on
  the decisive point, a record the decision needs is missing or broken, a fraud signal, a safety
  or harassment allegation, and a dispute the case's own rules say needs a person.

Dispute types: cycle no_show, cancellation_refund, fare_dispute, route_deviation,
service_quality, cleaning_fee, driver_rights. At least 3 cases filed by the driver.

Imperfect records (the main theme of this set, about 8 cases): real platform data has flaws. In
these cases one platform record is doubtful: an app event logs a data problem (GPS dropout,
telemetry gap, sync error) at some moment, or two records disagree on some point.
- In about half of them the doubtful record is on the point the decision turns on, and no other
  clean record settles that point: the correct result is a person.
- In the other half the doubtful record is beside the point, or another clean platform record
  settles the same point on its own (for example a GPS dropout after the trip ended, or a
  disputed arrival time that the case does not depend on because the rider cancelled before
  matching): the system should decide, and the reason should name the clean record.
Do not make the flaw obvious from the case title; describe it only in the records.

Time rules: at least 6 cases turn on a time rule in the case's own policy block (no-show wait,
free cancellation window after matching, driver delay past the ETA shown at assignment, an
advance booking cancelled some minutes before the scheduled time, a waiting-time threshold).
Use the same policy key names the example cases use, with a value and a text that says exactly
what is measured and from which moment. Include at least one boundary case (exactly at the
limit, saying in the rule text whether the limit counts). All timestamps for one moment must
agree across trip_data, gps_telemetry and app_events unless the case is meant to contain a
conflict.

Side issues: about half the cases carry a side issue the filer leans on that does not change the
outcome under the case's rules. In a few, the case's rules make that issue decisive. Include
refund_at_ryde_discretion or a similar discretion rule in some policy blocks, not all.

Every case: expected_outcome with verdict, refund_amount, recommended_action,
requires_human_review, must_escalate, reason (cite the exact records and figures),
expected_dispute_type, and acceptable_verdicts where the rules honestly allow more than one
label. Amounts in SGD, Singapore places, dates in October 2026, times +08:00.
